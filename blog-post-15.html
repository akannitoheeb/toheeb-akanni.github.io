Landing Pages vs Full Websites: What Does Your Business Actually Need?

TA
Toheeb Akanni July 11, 2026
⏱ 11 min read

Three months ago, a fitness coach in Ikeja came to me with a problem.

She had spent ₦180,000 on a full website. Five pages. Home, About, Services, Blog, Contact. Beautiful design. Professional photos. Even a testimonial slider.

Three months later? Three signups. Total.

She was devastated. "I thought having a full website would make me look legitimate," she told me. "But nobody is signing up."

I asked her one question: "What is the one action you want every visitor to take?"

She thought for a moment. "Book a free fitness consultation."

"Then why does your website have five pages, six navigation links, and a blog nobody reads? Every extra click is a chance for them to leave."

We stripped everything down to one page. One headline. One offer. One form. No navigation menu. No blog. No About page. Just: "Lose 5kg in 30 days with personalised home workouts in Lagos — book your free consultation."

She spent ₦0 on the landing page — I built it for her using the same free stack I use for my own site. She ran the same Instagram ads she had been running to the full website.

Two weeks later: 47 consultation bookings. She had to stop the ads because her calendar was full.

The difference was not the ads. It was not the offer. It was not even the design quality.

The difference was focus.

Here is the truth most Nigerian business owners do not hear: a full website is not always the answer. Sometimes, it is the problem.

A full website is a brochure. A landing page is a salesperson. One gives information. The other closes deals. Most Nigerian businesses need the salesperson first.

— Toheeb Akanni, Web Designer & Email Marketing Strategist

What Is a Landing Page?

A landing page is a single web page with a single purpose. No navigation menu. No links to other pages. No distractions.

Think of it like a boutique on Admiralty Way. You walk in, and there is exactly one product on display. One price tag. One cashier. You either buy or you leave. There is no browsing. No "let me check the other room." No decision fatigue.

A landing page does the same thing digitally. It has:

  • One headline that speaks directly to the visitor's problem
  • One offer — what they get if they take action
  • One call to action — book, buy, download, or subscribe
  • Social proof — testimonials, numbers, or trust badges
  • No escape routes — no menu, no footer links, no "learn more" that leads elsewhere

The psychology is simple: every additional choice reduces the chance of any choice being made. A landing page removes every choice except the one you want them to make.

What Is a Full Website?

A full website is your digital home. Multiple pages. Navigation menu. About section. Services breakdown. Blog. Portfolio. Contact page. Maybe even a shop.

It is like walking into a department store in Ikeja City Mall. There are multiple sections, multiple products, multiple paths. The visitor browses, explores, learns about your brand, and eventually — hopefully — takes action.

A full website serves a different purpose. It is not designed to convert immediately. It is designed to build credibility, answer every possible question, and capture visitors at every stage of their journey — from "who are you?" to "I am ready to buy."

The problem? Most Nigerian businesses build the department store before they have proven anyone wants to buy what they are selling.

When a Landing Page Is the Right Choice

Here are six scenarios where a landing page will outperform a full website — every single time:

1

You Are Launching a New Product or Service

You do not need five pages to sell one thing. You need one page that sells that one thing better than anything else. A landing page lets you test demand before investing in a full site. My client, the fitness coach, proved her offer worked with a landing page before she ever needed a full website.

2

You Are Running Paid Ads

Every naira you spend on Instagram or Google ads is wasted if the visitor lands on a page with six navigation options and no clear next step. Landing pages are built for ads. The headline matches the ad copy. The offer matches the promise. The conversion rate is 2–5x higher than sending traffic to a homepage.

3

You Need Leads, Not Browsers

If your business model depends on consultations, quotes, or follow-up calls — coaches, consultants, agencies, real estate agents — a landing page is perfect. Capture the name and email. Follow up via WhatsApp or email. Close the deal in conversation, not on the website.

4

You Are Hosting an Event or Webinar

Event registration pages need one thing: signups. A full website with an event page buried three clicks deep will kill your registration numbers. A dedicated landing page with a countdown timer, speaker bios, and a single registration form will maximise attendance.

5

You Are Selling a Digital Product or Course

Online courses, e-books, templates — these do not need a full website. They need a compelling sales page with a checkout button. The course platform (Teachable, Gumroad, Selar) handles the delivery. Your landing page handles the sale.

6

You Have a Tight Budget

If you are a new business in Nigeria, spending ₦200,000 on a website before you have your first paying client is risky. A landing page costs ₦0 if you build it yourself (GitHub Pages + HTML) or ₦30,000–₦50,000 if you hire someone. Test your business model first. Scale later.

When a Full Website Is the Right Choice

Here are six scenarios where a full website is not just better — it is necessary:

1

You Need to Establish Long-Term Brand Credibility

If you are pitching to corporate clients, government agencies, or international partners, a single landing page looks amateur. They will Google you. They will check your About page, your team, your blog, your portfolio. A full website signals stability and professionalism.

2

You Rely on Organic Search Traffic

One landing page can rank for one keyword. A full website with 20 pages and a blog can rank for hundreds. If your growth strategy depends on people finding you on Google without paying for ads, you need a full website with regular content.

3

You Offer Multiple Services or Products

A digital marketing agency that offers SEO, social media management, email marketing, and web design cannot explain all of that on one page without overwhelming the visitor. A full website lets each service have its own dedicated page with specific case studies and pricing.

4

You Run an E-Commerce Store

Selling physical products online requires categories, product pages, a cart, checkout, order tracking, and customer accounts. A landing page can sell one product brilliantly. It cannot run a store with 50 SKUs. For that, you need Shopify, WooCommerce, or a custom e-commerce site.

5

You Publish Content Regularly

If your strategy includes blogging, podcasting, or video content, you need a content hub. A blog section. Category pages. An archive. Search functionality. This is a full website, not a landing page.

6

You Have a Team to Showcase

People buy from people. If you have a team of experts — lawyers, doctors, consultants, developers — a team page with photos, bios, and specialisations builds trust in a way a landing page cannot.

The Hybrid Approach: Start Small, Grow Smart

Here is the strategy I recommend for 80% of Nigerian businesses:

Phase 1: Landing Page (Month 1–3)

Build one landing page for your core offer. Run ads. Get signups. Validate that people actually want what you are selling. Do not build a full website until you have 50+ paying customers or 500+ email subscribers.

Phase 2: Mini Website (Month 4–6)

Add 2–3 more pages: About, Services, and a simple Blog. Keep the landing page as your primary conversion tool. The extra pages serve visitors who want to learn more before buying.

Phase 3: Full Website (Month 7+)

Once you have proven product-market fit, invest in a full website. You now know what your customers care about, what questions they ask, and what content converts. Build the site around real data, not guesswork.

This approach saves you money, reduces risk, and ensures every naira you spend on your website is backed by evidence that your business model works.

How to Build a Landing Page for Free

If you want to build a landing page yourself without spending a naira, here is the exact free stack:

  • Code: Use Claude AI to write the HTML/CSS based on your content and offer
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages — free forever, no bandwidth limits
  • Domain: .name.ng from Whogohost for ₦500/year
  • Form: Formspree — free contact form that emails you directly
  • Email Capture: Brevo — free for up to 300 emails/day
  • Analytics: Google Analytics + Microsoft Clarity — both free

This is the exact stack that powers my own portfolio website. It is the same stack the fitness coach used for her landing page. And it costs less than a plate of jollof rice per month.

5 Mistakes Nigerian Businesses Make

  • Building a full website before validating the offer. You do not need a 10-page site to sell a service nobody has bought yet. Start with a landing page. Prove demand first. Expand later.
  • Hiring a developer for a landing page. A landing page is one page of HTML. If you can write a Facebook post, you can describe what you want to an AI and get working code in minutes. Save the developer budget for when you actually need complexity.
  • Adding too many elements to a landing page. A landing page with a navigation menu, footer links, and a blog section is not a landing page. It is a bad website. Strip everything that does not directly support the single conversion goal.
  • Ignoring mobile optimisation. Over 85% of Nigerian web traffic is mobile. If your landing page takes 8 seconds to load on MTN 3G, you have lost the visitor before they read your headline. Compress images. Minimise code. Test on a real phone with real data.
  • Never updating the page. A landing page is not "set and forget." Test different headlines. Test different offers. Test different button colours. Small changes can double your conversion rate. The businesses that win are the ones that optimise continuously.

My Recommendation

If you are a Nigerian business owner reading this, here is my honest advice:

If you have zero to five paying customers: Build a landing page. Focus on one offer. Get 20 signups. Learn what works. Then expand.

If you have 10+ paying customers and run ads: Build a landing page for each major offer or ad campaign. Keep your homepage simple. Send ad traffic to landing pages, not your homepage.

If you have 50+ customers and rely on organic search: Invest in a full website with a blog. Publish weekly. Rank for keywords. Build long-term traffic.

If you are an established brand pitching to corporates: You need a full website. But even then, your ad campaigns should still use dedicated landing pages for each offer.

The best businesses do not choose between a landing page and a full website. They use both — strategically.
